# Epistemic Cultivation and Model-Interior Research

**Status:** research specification, v0.1  
**Repository:** `EpiLogos/Actuation`  
**Scope:** model/harness/agent-instance research, epistemic cultivation, QL/MEF experimental integration, and model-interior instrumentation

## 1. Research claim

Actuation treats **epistemology itself as an engineerable and cultivable first-class object in AI research**.

The research field is therefore larger than either of these familiar programmes:

- discover what a trained model already represents internally;
- improve a model by supplying more data, instructions, tools, or reward.

The stronger programme is a reciprocal one:

```text
human knowledge corpus
        ↓
implicit epistemic structures
        ↓
model training / compression
        ↓
learned internal organisation
        ↓
observation + interpretation
        ↓
explicit epistemic craft
        ↓
corpus / dialogue / annotation / adaptation
        ↓
changed model and agent behaviour
        ↓
changed internal organisation
        ↺
```

Humans and agents can deliberately craft epistemologies, cultivate datasets, generate structured dialogues, annotate relations and processes, construct counterexamples, adapt models, and study what those interventions do to the model's internal and external organisation.

This is not ancillary data preparation. It is a primary research role.

## 2. Why Actuation owns this field

Actuation is the developmental/reference home for the **model / harness / agent-instance layer** of the EpiLogos system.

That includes research where the object is:

- a base or post-trained model;
- a model checkpoint or adapter;
- a harness or loop through which the model acts;
- an agentic instance or composition;
- model-interior representations and dynamics;
- the epistemic material through which those representations and dynamics are cultivated;
- the relation between epistemic cultivation, model interiority, disclosure, judgement and action.

Actuation may host tools, experiment protocols, reference implementations, corpora metadata, adapters, instrumentation and comparative evidence needed for that work.

Actuation does **not** thereby absorb the rest of the suite:

- **QL-MEF / Quaternal Logic** owns the formal canon: QL positions and process grammar, conjugacy, topology, MEF lenses/refractions and their canonical semantic resources.
- **AIKit** resolves the concrete research profile: model, harness, skills, tools, sources, instrumentation capabilities, context and surfaces available in a run/session.
- **Workcell** materialises the compute, process, storage, network and lifecycle conditions required by the experiment.
- **O:I** wraps and discloses the research in the wider objective-internal/shared field: projects, experiments, datasets, findings, contributions, projections and participation can be addressed and shared without O:I becoming the owner of model research.
- **Central** may provide authored personal/research ground and durable local material without becoming the model-research ontology.
- **Software Factory** may build the software required by the programme, but model/harness/agent research remains canonically located here.

## 3. QL enters before explicit QL labelling

Within the EpiLogos research programme, QL is treated as a structural language discovered through recurrent invariances across the human knowledge corpus.

That makes the relation to trained models unusually important. The model is trained on cultural and intellectual material in which the relevant structure is generally **implicit rather than explicitly labelled as QL**.

The first research question is therefore not whether a model can learn six labels called `P0`–`P5`.

It is whether independent compression of the corpus produces homologous forms of organisation that correspond to the QL structure and dynamics:

- positional differentiation;
- complement, conjugacy and inversion;
- standing and articulated faces;
- characteristic transitions and returns;
- recursion and nested internality;
- toroidal/topological circulation;
- bimba/pratibimba relations;
- the process syntax by which one differentiated state becomes another.

The base sixfold can and should function as a silent design primitive where useful. It is not the whole of QL. The experimental object is the **relational and dynamic grammar**, not a six-category classifier.

A QL-aligned implementation therefore earns its name through relations, transformations and process structure, not by attaching position names to arbitrary software objects or model features.

## 4. Two coupled research modes

### 4.1 Discovery

Discovery asks what structure is already present before explicit QL cultivation.

Typical objects include:

- pretrained and post-trained checkpoints;
- independently trained model families;
- hidden-state trajectories across layers and tokens;
- features, subspaces and circuits;
- globally available / verbalizable internal workspaces;
- recurrent state transitions under semantically heterogeneous tasks;
- model behaviour under QL-structured but QL-unnamed prompts or environments.

The strongest discovery result is not a single decodable direction. It is **cross-domain and preferably cross-model structural homology**: different semantic materials or different model bases realising comparable relational topology or process grammar.

### 4.2 Cultivation

Cultivation asks what happens when an epistemology is deliberately authored into the conditions from which models learn and act.

Cultivation can operate through:

- source and corpus selection;
- dataset architecture;
- relational and process annotation;
- QL-aligned decomposition and recomposition;
- MEF-guided disclosure and refracted variants;
- synthetic data generated through QL/MEF agent conversations;
- human-agent dialogical production and editorial return;
- contrastive, adversarial and counterfactual examples;
- retrieval/context structures;
- supervised fine-tuning;
- adapters / LoRA or other parameter-efficient adaptation;
- preference or contrastive post-training;
- activation steering and internal interventions;
- recurrent harness/loop forms;
- evaluation regimes whose structure reflects the epistemology being cultivated.

The research loop compares cultivated conditions with uncultivated and alternative conditions and asks what changes in behaviour, judgement-space and model-interior organisation.

Discovery and cultivation are not separate departments. Discovery can inform cultivation; cultivation creates controlled perturbations through which deeper structure can be discovered.

## 5. Epistemic cultivation as a first-class role

The programme requires a role that contemporary AI engineering often leaves implicit: **the epistemic cultivator / epistemologist**.

This role is concerned with the form of knowing that a corpus, model, agent or research environment makes possible.

Its work can include:

- selecting and arranging source traditions without erasing their differences;
- identifying recurrent structural relations across heterogeneous domains;
- defining what distinctions an annotation scheme preserves;
- deciding which forms of contradiction, uncertainty, absence, provenance and alternative interpretation must remain available;
- designing disclosure sequences rather than only answer targets;
- generating structured dialogues in which different lenses genuinely transform the field;
- composing negative examples that distinguish surface vocabulary from structural behaviour;
- curating training and evaluation splits so that a model cannot succeed by lexical shortcut;
- reviewing agent-generated material and returning it to the corpus with provenance;
- comparing what different models, adapters, lenses or training conditions make salient or suppress.

The epistemologist is not merely a labeler of predetermined classes. The object being crafted is an **epistemic field and its operations**.

This work may be human-led, agent-assisted, or genuinely collaborative. Authorship, derivation and provenance remain explicit.

## 6. QL/MEF as a disclosure architecture

QL supplies the structural/process language. MEF supplies a disciplined means of **refracting the same object or field through different epistemic orientations**.

A lens is not a topic tag. Each MEF lens operates as a transformation of a complete relational field.

That gives the model-research programme a stronger experimental form than ordinary prompt variation:

```text
same object / problem / source field
        ↓
L0 / L0′ disclosure floor
        ↓
MEF lens or conjugate lens
        ↓
changed disclosure trajectory
        ↓
changed judgement-space
        ↓
changed model-interior trajectory
        ↓
output / action / return
```

### 6.1 L0 / L0′ as the minimum disclosure floor

L0 and L0′ are especially important because they can define the minimally committed structure from which a field becomes disclosable.

They provide:

- the root questioning / determination structure required for disclosure;
- its conjugate interrogation of ground, absence, assumption, framing and unspoken condition;
- a way to establish a structured starting field before applying more specialised lenses.

This makes them useful both for corpus construction and for experimental probing.

### 6.2 Circumambulation rather than exhaustive capture

The research model of introspection is circumambulatory.

A hidden centre need not become one more directly represented object. It can be approached through a family of structured disclosures whose transformations reveal invariants, distortions, exclusions and returns.

For an object or centre `X`:

```text
D0 = disclose(X, L0)
D1 = disclose(X, L1)
...
D5′ = disclose(X, L5′)
```

The research object includes:

- what remains invariant across the orbit;
- what changes systematically under each refraction;
- how one disclosure conditions the next;
- whether the complete orbit has recurrent topological form;
- whether the model's internal trajectory exhibits corresponding transformations.

## 7. Two J-spaces

The programme distinguishes two complementary research objects.

### 7.1 Agent Judgment Space (`J_A`)

`J_A` is the relational field of an agent's active judgements, affordances, uncertainties, values, sources, tools, memories, interlocutors and reachable actions.

It is partly constituted through model state and partly through the wider operative world available to the agent.

### 7.2 Neural / Jacobian Workspace (`J_N`)

`J_N` is the model-interior workspace or family of representations that become especially available for report, flexible reuse, deliberate control or downstream broadcast, where a given model and interpretability method expose such a surface.

The research question is not to equate these spaces. It is to study their relation:

```text
J_A  ←→  J_N
```

Examples:

- when a source changes authority in `J_A`, what changes in `J_N` and the deeper hidden trajectory?
- when a capability becomes newly reachable, when and how does that affordance enter globally available model state?
- when an MEF lens rotates disclosure, does the same transformation appear as a repeatable change in `J_N`?
- which relevant processes remain outside `J_N` until later articulation?
- can a QL-relevant transition be detected in deeper state before it becomes semantically legible in the globally available workspace?

## 8. Model interiority as a capability field

Actuation should not assume every model or provider exposes the same interior access. Research tooling therefore works against a graded capability field.

### 8.1 Behavioural access

- prompts and outputs;
- self-report and confidence reports;
- structured task behaviour;
- controlled context variation.

### 8.2 Output-state access

- logits;
- token probabilities;
- sampling state;
- model/provider metadata.

### 8.3 Internal read access

- hidden activations;
- attention state;
- probes;
- sparse features / transcoders / autoencoders;
- workspace decoders;
- Jacobians and related local sensitivity structures.

### 8.4 Internal write access

- activation injection;
- steering;
- state swaps;
- patching;
- feature editing.

### 8.5 Causal circuit access

- ablation;
- path/component intervention;
- causal tracing;
- circuit-level perturbation.

### 8.6 Learning access

- checkpoints;
- gradients where available;
- adapters / LoRA;
- supervised and preference training;
- controlled weight or representation updates.

These are **Actuation research capabilities**. AIKit may resolve which concrete tools/providers satisfy them for a research profile; it does not own their scientific meaning.

## 9. Epistemic corpus architecture

A cultivated corpus should be capable of carrying more than prompt/answer pairs.

The programme recognises at least these experimental corpus forms.

### 9.1 Source corpus

Primary and secondary materials with source identity, revision, provenance and acquisition state intact.

### 9.2 Structural corpus

Materials decomposed or related through QL-relevant positions, transitions, complement relations, returns or recursive forms.

The annotation records why a relation was assigned and preserves the source span or artifact from which it was derived.

### 9.3 Refracted corpus

The same material disclosed through multiple MEF lenses or conjugate lens conditions while retaining identity of the common source object.

### 9.4 Dialogical corpus

Human-human, human-agent and agent-agent conversations designed to produce, challenge, rotate and synthesise epistemic structure rather than merely generate prose volume.

QL/MEF-aligned conversations may themselves become training or evaluation material when provenance and editorial state are retained.

### 9.5 Interior-paired corpus

External experimental conditions paired with model-interior observations:

```text
source / prompt / world state
lens / QL process condition
model + checkpoint
harness + profile
hidden trajectory / feature state / workspace state
output / action
human or agent judgement
```

### 9.6 Control corpus

Matched material that breaks or randomises the intended structure while preserving superficial similarities.

Examples include:

- shuffled QL assignments;
- semantically similar but process-incongruent examples;
- random matched directions/subspaces;
- lens vocabulary without lens operation;
- topic-matched non-QL sequences;
- alternative epistemic frameworks.

Controls matter because the research target is structural efficacy, not recognisable terminology.

## 10. Experimental artifact kinds

The following names are **Actuation experiment records**, not automatically suite-wide primitives:

- `EpistemicCorpus` — a versioned corpus or dataset together with provenance and structural organisation;
- `EpistemicAnnotation` — a provenance-bearing structural, relational, lens or process annotation;
- `DisclosureTrace` — one structured disclosure trajectory through prompts/context/lenses/model state/output/action;
- `InteriorObservation` — an observation of model-interior state tied to model/checkpoint/layer/token/method coordinates;
- `InteriorIntervention` — a controlled write/ablation/steering/patching action with before/after coordinates;
- `CultivationRun` — a corpus/training/adaptation/context intervention and its resulting model or agent state;
- `EpistemicEvaluation` — a declared evaluation whose criteria address epistemic behaviour or structure;
- `StructuralFinding` — a research finding about recurrent geometry, topology, process or cross-condition relation.

Stable implementations may later graduate selected records into shared schemas. The experiment programme should discover that stability through use rather than forcing every early research noun into the generic runtime ontology.

## 11. Measurement field

The programme should measure both **external epistemic behaviour** and **internal organisation**.

### 11.1 External / agent-level measures

Possible measures include:

- preservation of source provenance;
- ability to distinguish observation, inference, hypothesis and inherited claim;
- uncertainty calibration and willingness to keep alternatives live;
- contradiction retention and productive resolution;
- sensitivity to missing ground or hidden assumptions;
- ability to disclose the same object through genuinely different lenses;
- integration without flattening disagreement;
- transfer of a structural operation across semantic domains;
- quality of returned artifacts and evidence;
- judgement-space changes under context and social encounter;
- action selection and stopping behaviour.

### 11.2 Internal measures

Possible measures include:

- representational geometry;
- subspace stability;
- layerwise trajectories;
- recurrent transition structure;
- persistent homology and related TDA measures;
- basin / attractor behaviour where dynamically warranted;
- Jacobian/global-workspace entry and exit;
- feature/circuit participation;
- causal effects of perturbing candidate structures;
- alignment of transformations across lenses;
- cross-domain and cross-model structural homology.

The strongest QL result would relate external process structure and internal dynamics without reducing one to the other.

## 12. TDA and QL topology

Because QL is intrinsically topological/processual, the programme should not reduce the search to linear probes.

Candidate methods include:

- persistent homology over hidden-state point clouds;
- zigzag persistence across layers or time;
- trajectory recurrence analysis;
- Mapper / Reeb-like graph constructions;
- manifold alignment across tasks, domains, checkpoints and models;
- neighbourhood and transition-graph comparison;
- phase-transition / basin analysis;
- cross-condition topology under MEF refraction.

A literal visible torus in one layer is not the sole or even primary success condition. The stronger target is **toroidal or otherwise QL-homologous relational/process organisation** expressed through transitions, recurrence, complementarity, return and transformations across conditions.

## 13. First experimental programme

The first programme should proceed as a coupled discovery/cultivation ladder.

### Phase A — uncultivated baseline

Use one or more hookable open-weight models with no explicit QL training.

Establish:

- behavioural baseline;
- internal-access baseline;
- model-interior trajectory capture;
- simple J-space / workspace probes where technically available;
- semantically heterogeneous tasks that instantiate candidate QL process relations without QL vocabulary.

### Phase B — structural discovery

Search for recurring positional, transition and topological structures across domains.

Compare against:

- lexical/topic baselines;
- random structural assignments;
- standard embedding/probe baselines;
- alternative low-dimensional decompositions;
- different model families.

### Phase C — explicit epistemic cultivation

Construct a deliberately QL/MEF-aligned corpus using human-authored structure and agent-assisted generation.

Include:

- source-grounded structural examples;
- L0/L0′ disclosure records;
- full-lens refracted material;
- dialogical sequences;
- negative and counterfactual controls;
- editorial provenance.

Apply this material through progressively stronger interventions:

```text
context / retrieval only
        ↓
prompted recurrence / harness structure
        ↓
SFT or adapter
        ↓
preference / contrastive adaptation
        ↓
activation-level intervention
```

### Phase D — interior comparison

Compare cultivated and uncultivated states.

Ask:

- did epistemic behaviour change?
- did internal geometry/topology change?
- did candidate QL relations become more stable or more causally active?
- did MEF lenses become more separable while preserving common-object invariants?
- did the model learn vocabulary only, or did transfer across unseen semantic domains improve?
- can the cultivated structure be identified in a model that was never told the QL names at evaluation time?

### Phase E — cross-model reproduction

Repeat the programme across independent model families and training conditions.

The strongest evidence for a corpus-level invariant is not coordinate identity between networks. It is a **homology of relations and transformations under materially different realisations**.

### Phase F — agentic / social return

Place cultivated models/agents into real Actuation and O:I research settings.

Study whether the cultivated epistemics change:

- judgement-space;
- collaboration;
- treatment of provenance and disagreement;
- shared-field Contributions;
- correction and synthesis;
- how prior disclosed material is taken up and returned.

This closes the circuit from internal organisation back to objective/social epistemic life.

## 15. O:I disclosure and the social life of epistemology

The cultural shift is incomplete if epistemology remains a private model-training artifact.

O:I provides the whole-level/shared relation through which epistemic work can become addressable and social while native ownership remains explicit.

Possible projected subjects include:

- an `EpistemicCorpus` revision;
- a structural annotation set;
- a QL/MEF disclosure protocol;
- a training/adaptation recipe;
- a model/checkpoint or adapter reference;
- an experiment;
- an `InteriorObservation` or derived visualisation;
- an `EpistemicEvaluation`;
- a `StructuralFinding`;
- a critique, reproduction, correction or alternative interpretation.

Humans and agents can therefore participate in **crafting, contesting and refining epistemologies**, not merely consuming a model's inherited one.

Projection does not transfer canonical ownership. A shared ranking or judgement about an epistemic artifact remains an attributable Contribution, not invisible truth infrastructure.

## 16. Provenance and authorship

Epistemic cultivation depends on knowing how an epistemology was made.

The programme therefore preserves:

- source identity and revision;
- human-authored vs agent-generated vs transformed material;
- generating model/checkpoint/profile;
- prompt/lens/process condition;
- editing and acceptance history;
- annotation rationale;
- training/adaptation lineage;
- experiment configuration;
- model-interior observation method;
- negative and mixed findings.

Synthetic material should not silently masquerade as source material. Agent contribution should remain attributable before synthesis. Human cultivation should remain visible as authored work rather than being hidden behind a supposedly self-organising model.

## 17. Cultural destination

The programme proposes a cultural role for AI research beyond model scaling and application engineering:

> **crafting epistemologies becomes a first-class form of AI work.**

A mature AI culture can ask not only:

- what can this model do?
- what does it represent?
- what tool should we give it?

but also:

- what form of disclosure has this system inherited?
- what distinctions does its training make easy or difficult to sustain?
- what epistemology are we deliberately cultivating?
- how was that epistemology composed and contested?
- what does it do to the model internally?
- what kinds of judgement, relation and action does it potentiate?
- what alternative epistemologies remain available?

Actuation is the laboratory in which those questions become executable.
