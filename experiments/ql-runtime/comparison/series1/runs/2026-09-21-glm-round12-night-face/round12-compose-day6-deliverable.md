# Answerability — what binds a society, and what should govern it

A society is not held together by agreement. Ask what a people actually share and you find fault lines: of faith, of interest, of memory, of hope. Any bond that requires consensus breaks on the first serious disagreement — and disagreement is not an accident to be repaired but the permanent condition of lives lived in plural.

Nor is a society held together by force. Force can produce compliance, and compliance can look like order, but compliance is not union: the coerced remain a crowd, held only while the holding lasts. A bond that exists wherever enforcement happens to be is not a bond.

What binds is humbler and stranger: mutual exposure become mutual claim. We are bound because we can hurt each other and be hurt, because our fates run through shared water, shared markets, shared air, shared law. That exposure is the raw material. A society exists wherever it has been turned into something owed rather than merely suffered — into the promise, the contract, the vote, the tax, the right of way. Each is interdependence given a shape in which it can be honored, disputed, and repaired. Strip the shapes away and the exposure does not vanish; it returns as fear and domination.

If that is the bond, this should govern: answerability. Not the strongest interest, which mistakes society for its capture; not tradition as such, which mistakes the bond for its crust. Power should govern only insofar as it must give public reasons to those who bear its consequences, and those who bear them must have standing to demand reasons and to refuse. Institutions, on this view, are not neutral machinery; they are the memory of that discipline. Constitutions, courts, a press free to antagonize, the right to associate and to withhold labor — each keeps answerability from being forgotten between elections. Corruption is that forgetting made routine.

Justice, then, is not first a pattern of holdings. It is a condition of relations: that no one's dependence on others is left without standing. A society is just to the degree that no one faces power only as a supplicant, and no one meets others only as a master. Shared values matter, but they are the yield of such relations, not their seed: where answerability is practiced, agreement grows as fruit, not as gatekeeping.

What binds us: acknowledged dependence. What should govern: the discipline of being answerable for it.
