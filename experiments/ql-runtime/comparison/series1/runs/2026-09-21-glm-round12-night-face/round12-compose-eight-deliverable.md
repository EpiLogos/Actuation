# What Holds: A Political Philosophy of Practiced Bonds

A society is not held together by blood, creed, contract, or conquest. These are the names we give to bonds after they exist. What actually binds is thinner and more demanding: practiced interdependence — the ongoing activity of needing, trusting, and answering one another. Remove it and every institution remains standing on paper while the society beneath them quietly dissolves.

This means cohesion is not a possession but a process. There is no stock of unity a founding moment can bank; there is only flow — bonds renewed or neglected, day by day. Trust circulates or it clots. Recognition is extended or it narrows. A society is therefore never finished being bound; it is bound only while the binding continues.

From this one claim, everything about governance follows. What should govern is not a sovereign standing above the bonds, nor a market left to connect us anonymously, but whatever keeps the binding in motion. Good institutions are those that convert conflict into shared governance: they take the disagreements that would otherwise fracture us and give them a form in which answering each other becomes the ordinary way of doing things. Courts, elections, assemblies, unions, commons — they matter not for their shapes but for their function: they metabolize conflict into renewed interdependence.

Legitimacy, likewise, is not a source but an achievement. Power is legitimate exactly insofar as it is exercised through participation — people recognize a rule because they can see themselves in it, and can contest it without breaking the bond. Coercion can compel compliance, but compliance is not the same as being bound; a regime of pure force governs a crowd, not a society. Force has a place only as scaffold — protecting the processes of mutual recognition — and it corrodes everything the moment it substitutes for consent, cannibalizing the very bonds it claims to protect.

The test for any arrangement, then, is a single question: does it reproduce our capacity to bind one another? Widen participation, circulate trust, keep conflict speakable — and it belongs. Hollow the bonds while keeping order — and it is dissolution wearing order's face.

A society, in the end, is whole only while it is being bound. Governance is the art of keeping that binding alive — never its replacement.
