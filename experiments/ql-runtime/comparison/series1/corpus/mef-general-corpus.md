# MEF general corpus — real positions and common notions across domains (2026-09-21)

Test material for the MEF/jev analytical reading: **real arguments and common
notions, general across domains** — deliberately NOT the lens-source
traditions (no Aristotle, Nāgārjuna, Heidegger, Whitehead, Hegel, Kashmir
Shaivism, Jung, Kuhn texts; the lenses' own inspirations are excluded so the
readings cannot be self-answering). Public-domain statements quoted where
they exist; modern positions stated as precise cited paraphrases.

Each entry: the statement the instrument reads, and the argument's core move
(for comparison against what the readings claim).

---

## C1. Utilitarianism — Bentham (1789), IPML ch. I

> "Nature has placed mankind under the governance of two sovereign masters,
> pain and pleasure. It is for them alone to point out what we ought to do,
> as well as to determine what we shall do."

Core move: reduce right action to the aggregation of pleasure over pain;
value is a measurable sum.

## C2. The invisible hand — Smith (1776), Wealth of Nations IV.2

> "It is not from the benevolence of the butcher, the brewer, or the baker
> that we expect our dinner, but from their regard to their own interest…
> he intends only his own gain, and he is in this… led by an invisible hand
> to promote an end which was no part of his intention."

Core move: decentralized self-interest produces coordinated public benefit
without intention or design.

## C3. Entitlement theory — Nozick (1974), Anarchy, State, and Utopia ch. 7

Precise paraphrase: whatever distribution arises from just acquisitions and
just voluntary transfers (plus rectification of past injustice) is itself
just — no pattern (equality, need, merit) may be imposed without violating
someone's entitlements. "Liberty upsets patterns."

Core move: historical entitlement replaces patterned justice; imposing a
pattern requires continuous interference with free exchanges.

## C4. The partnership of generations — Burke (1790), Reflections

> "Society is indeed a contract… but the state ought not to be considered as
> nothing better than a partnership agreement in a trade of pepper and
> coffee… It is a partnership in all science; a partnership in all art; a
> partnership in every virtue… not only between those who are living, but
> between those who are living, those who are dead, and those who are to be
> born."

Core move: political obligation extends across time; the present holds the
order in trust, not as owners.

## C5. Government by consent — Locke (1689), Second Treatise §§87, 95, 124

> "Men being… by nature all free, equal and independent, no one can be…
> subjected to the political power of another, without his own consent…
> The great and chief end, therefore, of men's uniting into commonwealths,
> and putting themselves under government, is the preservation of their
> property."

Core move: legitimate power is fiduciary, delegated by natural-rights
holders for a limited end; it reverts when the end is betrayed.

## C6. Historical materialism — Marx (1859), Preface to A Contribution

> "It is not the consciousness of men that determines their existence, but
> their social existence that determines their consciousness. At a certain
> stage of development, the material productive forces of society come into
> conflict with the existing relations of production… Then begins an era of
> social revolution."

Core move: the economic base determines the superstructure; change is driven
by forces/relations contradiction, not ideas.

## C7. The tragedy of the commons — Hardin (1968), Science

> "Ruin is the destination toward which all men rush, each pursuing his own
> best interest in a society that believes in the freedom of the commons.
> Freedom in a commons brings ruin to all."

Core move: individually rational use of an unowned shared resource aggregate
to collective catastrophe; the solution is "mutual coercion, mutually agreed
upon."

## C8. Two systems of thinking — Kahneman (2011), Thinking, Fast and Slow

Precise paraphrase: cognition runs on two modes — System 1, fast, automatic,
associative, effortless; System 2, slow, deliberate, rule-following,
effortful. Most judgment is System 1's output post-rationalized; predictable
biases follow from each system's signature failures.

Core move: split the mind's work between automatic and controlled processes,
and locate specific errors in specific systems.

## C9. Linguistic relativity — Whorf (1940)

> "We dissect nature along lines laid down by our native languages… the
> world is presented in a kaleidoscopic flux of impressions which has to be
> organized by our minds — and this means largely by the linguistic systems
> in our minds."

Core move: the categories of one's language shape what one can perceive and
think; speakers of different languages live in different world-structures.

## C10. Sense and reference — Frege (1892), Über Sinn und Bedeutung

> "The morning star and the evening star are the same body — and yet the
> thought expressed by 'the morning star is the morning star' differs from
> that expressed by 'the morning star is the evening star.'"

Core move: meaning (Sinn) and reference (Bedeutung) come apart; identity
statements can be informative because a term carries more than what it
points at.

## C11. The unconscious — Freud (1915), The Unconscious

Precise paraphrase: mental life includes processes actively kept outside
consciousness by repression; these are organized, aim-directed, and return
in symptoms, slips, and dreams — the conscious mind is not the master of its
own house.

Core move: explain manifest irrationality as the return of a structured,
hidden mental process rather than as randomness.

## C12. Probability as degree of belief — Ramsey (1926), Truth and Probability

Precise paraphrase: probability is not a frequency in the world but a
rational agent's degree of belief, measurable by the bets they will accept;
consistency across bets is the whole of probability theory.

Core move: subject the logic of uncertainty to the agent's dispositions to
act, and derive its axioms from coherence under betting.
